Every time the fox in *The Little Prince* tells his secret, it stirs up a storm inside me. It keeps making me rethink what I'm taming, or what's taming me.

The fox's secret is a simple one. You can only see clearly with your heart, because "what is essential is invisible to the eye." The little prince repeats each thing the fox tells him so he'll be sure to remember it: that the time he spent on his rose is what makes her so important, that people have forgotten this truth but he mustn't, and that you are responsible, forever, for what you have tamed. He is responsible for his rose.

![Curtain call of CMusical's The Little Prince, performers with arms outstretched in front of a starry set](/writing/vocus/what-is-essential-is-invisible/01.jpg)

*CMusical's The Little Prince*

I went to the theater to see the musical adaptation, thinking about my teammates the whole time. Out of the 80-minute show, I was probably choked up for something like 40 minutes. *The Little Prince* keeps making me think about myself all over again, as an adult and as a kid, from every angle, and it never comes with any sense of judgment. It gives me a chance to rest and have a real conversation with my own heart.

[▶ YouTube](https://youtu.be/cHKoOGDM8Fc)

Today, after searching for ages, I finally found a fairly beginner-friendly camera test from a YouTuber. This camera is going to be our main workhorse as we push on with our mission work and at the retreat center. On top of already being a Netflix-approved cinema camera, it's one I always look forward to taking out on industry shoots, so I'm really happy the church was able to buy one as our main camera!

So I'd love for my teammates to take a look at it, so we can all start getting excited about it. The main part is around the five-minute mark of the video, where it talks specifically about focus. That's something our old EFP studio cameras just couldn't do. Of course, I also really hope everyone makes good use of Sony's built-in, top-of-the-line focusing and keeps conquering the services at Bread of Life Christian Church in Taipei!

I'm also looking forward to chatting with everyone a bit about focus. In Mandarin, "getting focused" is also office talk for getting everyone aligned in a meeting, and we all do plenty of that at our own posts. But in cinematography, focus is a hugely important part of the job, because it chooses what we give the audience. I can emphasize a musician's strings, or their fingers. That's something we'll really need to feel out on site when we shoot, because focus can tell so many "things the eyes can't see."

Our goal is to "help people draw near to God." By shooting in a way that feels as present in the room as possible, I really hope even friends who can't make it in person can feel God's presence. So I want to ask my teammates: the God you picture, what does He look like?

We all have five senses, and some of us have senses that are especially sharp, so we come to know God through them. We try, as best we can, to recreate our own experience of meeting God, creating in the same time and space as the worship team. They use instruments and voices. We use the camera and focus. Once the frame is composed, what's left for us is deciding where the focus goes.

I think it's the same as always: for those of us filming worship, there may not be a right answer, because it all depends on that moment, on how you're expecting God to speak to you, or to the viewer, rather than on what we work out in our heads. In my own experience, though, He doesn't show up as a physical image. He shows up as a nudge, an impression. That impression tells me to look more in a certain direction, or to adjust the framing a little, and inside that frame I can feel the impression and me meeting. What about you, team? How do you do it? I'd really love for you to share.

Last of all, I still want to say this: on this road of making images, I don't think there's any one right answer. Let's hold on to why we started, and keep protecting the things that matter most deep in our hearts!
