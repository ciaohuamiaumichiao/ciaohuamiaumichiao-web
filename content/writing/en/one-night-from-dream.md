Back when we were kids chasing dreams, most of us probably watched *October Sky* at school at some point. Besides being adapted from a book, it's a true story. [Homer Hickam](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%8D%B7%E9%BB%98%C2%B7%E5%B8%8C%E5%9D%8E%E5%A7%86) and his band of rocket boys slowly became a kind of subconscious presence for everyone trying to chase a dream.

![Banner poster for October Sky, two figures looking up at the stars and the moon](/writing/vocus/one-night-from-dream/01.jpg)

I always assumed that once the turbulence of youth was over, we'd each head off into our own lives and look back on those years with a smile.

Twenty years later, the boys chasing dreams have become middle-aged men chasing dreams. In *The Astronaut* (勇闖太空), Nicolas Giraud plays one of them. In the script, he's a systems engineer at a space company who still carries the disappointment of not being selected as an astronaut by the European Space Agency. Step by step, he follows the small dream in his heart until he's built his own space program.

![Taiwanese poster for The Astronaut, a close-up of a face inside a space helmet](/writing/vocus/one-night-from-dream/02.jpg)

Courtesy of Applause Entertainment

It's hard not to be drawn to space. In *The Astronaut*, aerospace engineer Jim (Nicolas Giraud) methodically plans to send himself into space. He pulls together a pyrotechnics chemist from an amateur club, a retired astronaut, a mathematician with no formal degree, and his own grandmother. They solve one problem after another, until the dream finally comes true.

![Still from The Astronaut, the five-person team standing side by side looking ahead](/writing/vocus/one-night-from-dream/03.jpg)

Courtesy of Applause Entertainment

For me, this film left one feeling very deep: "The distance between a dream and its coming true is just one night." A dream is that close. So close that once you start chasing it, it comes to find you the very next day.

A lot of the conflicts the film sets up get resolved the way Jim himself would resolve them: by waiting things out. But at every crucial moment, Jim the dreamer doesn't hide his vulnerability either, or else he throws himself into shouldering the responsibility.

The twist after the dream comes true is more than a surprise. It's full of warmth, and it left me with that real sense of fullness you get when a dream has actually been achieved. In the final scene in space, the way it's shot made the whole theater feel like it had passed through the atmosphere, every one of us in the audience lying in Earth's embrace alongside the main character. That feeling stayed with me long after the film was over.

![Still from The Astronaut, the main character in a space helmet looking up](/writing/vocus/one-night-from-dream/04.jpg)

Courtesy of Applause Entertainment

Most of us have some kind of movie budget every month or every quarter, and we tend to spend it where it counts, on the most immersive big-screen experience we can get. But I sincerely recommend treating yourself to a French romantic dinner once in a while, and enjoying a film where every composition and every shift of light is pure photographic beauty!

*The Astronaut* was nominated for the Audience Award at the Glasgow Film Festival and for Best Film at the Cairo International Film Festival. It follows aerospace engineer Jim, who has always dreamed of becoming an astronaut. Unable to let that dream go, he flies into space in a mini rocket he built entirely by hand, and steps out of the capsule for a spacewalk. The film finds its own path within science fiction and writes a space poem for everyone. It brings together space, rockets, science, dreams and a band of like-minded friends helping each other, and it's inspiring and moving.

*The Astronaut* (L'Astronaute)

Drama | France | In French | 110 minutes | Released in Taiwan on August 18, 2023

★ Nominated, Best Film, Cairo International Film Festival ★ Nominated, Audience Award, Glasgow Film Festival ★ Nicolas Giraud (*High Lane*) ★ Mathieu Kassovitz (*The Wolf's Call*)

An impossible team of five risks everything for a walk in space

Brilliant aerospace engineer Jim has dreamed of becoming an astronaut since he was a boy, but missed out in the European Space Agency selection despite a high score. Refusing to give up, he has spent years buried in a secret project: he plans to fly into space in a mini rocket he designed and built himself, then step out of the capsule for a spacewalk. But adventure is one thing, and getting yourself into space is another. To make his dream real, he puts together a team of five who throw everything they have into this death-defying plan. One problem follows another. Can this impossible mission really succeed?
