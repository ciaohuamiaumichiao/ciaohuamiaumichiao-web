Around that time my workouts were going really badly. My body wasn't tired and my strength was all still there. I just couldn't get myself going. I'd walk into the gym in the morning after a full night's sleep, look at the equipment, and my hands just didn't want to reach out. Eventually I had to come clean to my trainer: I can't lift lately.

My trainer isn't the type who stands next to you yelling "Come on, push past your limits!" He's more like a mix of massage therapist, cupping guy and traditional Chinese medicine doctor. That day he didn't ask anything else. He took me over to a table and said, "Lie down."

Then he started cupping. From the back of my neck all the way down my spine, and in the end he did my stomach too. I said, wait, you can do the stomach? It's all fat in there. He completely ignored me. Halfway through he suddenly said, "I'm going to open up your microcosmic orbit." I said, please, that's from a Jin Yong kung fu novel. He answered, very seriously, "It's real."

When he was done, I had about 15 reddish-purple circles on me. That afternoon I went to train, and a 95 kg deadlift was so easy it even stopped me for a second. It was like lifting an empty bar.

## On the way home, I kept thinking

Does cupping really work that well? Or was it all in my head? I thought about it the whole way, and what eventually made more sense was that the thing holding me back wasn't in my back at all. Back then my head was going round and round: no projects lately, money's a bit short, what about next month, what am I even doing. Leave that stuff piled up long enough and it turns into a body that doesn't want to move, and no amount of eating and sleeping helps.

Maybe that round of cupping really did loosen some muscle. But what I remember most clearly is "lie down, I'll handle it." Someone had taken over, and suddenly that stuck feeling became something that could be dealt with.

I was busy every day back then, but what I was doing was scattered, and looking back I didn't really know why I was doing it. If I knew I had to write 5 articles and shoot 1 sample film this month, at least each day I could tell myself I'd moved forward a little. But with no goals at all, every night I'd feel like I'd wasted another day, and I still couldn't say what I was supposed to be doing.

Then again, life isn't supposed to have a clear direction at every stage. I've spent a long time walking around in the haze myself. Every year I think this year will be clearer, and it's the same as always, and then another year goes by. So if I waited until things were clear before setting off, I'd probably be waiting forever. What I do more often is one small thing first: write one line, take one photo, make one phone call, run 500 meters, clean for 10 minutes. Once I've done it, the next thing gets a little easier. Testing things out with your body really is much, much faster than sitting there thinking.

Are you stuck somewhere lately too? Want to pick something ridiculously small and just try doing it?

In the days after the cupping, I deadlifted 95, wrote 3 articles and answered five emails. I'm 41 and my direction still isn't all that clear, but that week, I was moving.
