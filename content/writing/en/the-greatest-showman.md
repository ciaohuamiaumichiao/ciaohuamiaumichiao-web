> "Since when has happiness ever been boring?" (Charity)

Three viewings in seven days. Thanks to Disney+, my family and I can rewatch this classic as many times as we want. My kid's recital is coming up, and they'll be singing "The Greatest Show" and "This Is Me," two huge, ridiculously stirring songs that work on any stage. So we figured, why not just watch the movie?

![Still from The Greatest Showman: the ringmaster and the circus troupe together on stage](/writing/vocus/the-greatest-showman/01.jpg)

## A box office surprise

*The Greatest Showman* came out in 2017. It premiered aboard the Queen Mary 2 before opening in theaters across the US. The first week didn't go as well as hoped, and the box office didn't even reach ten million. That may have had something to do with the negative image Americans grow up with of the real man the film is about. Then, in its second week, it took off, selling close to twice what it did in the first.

It was also Michael Gracey's first feature as a director. Before that he'd done visual effects on a lot of films, and he was very good at music videos and commercials. Fox brought him on in 2012 to make this film, and the result is remarkably mature. Personally, I have nothing but respect and admiration for it. You can basically think of it as a very smooth compilation of music videos, except, unfortunately for the rest of us, it's a compilation at the level of the Hollywood film industry. So you get top actors, real musical theater performers, real dancers, and of course a very strong team of writers and producers. Oh, right, and real trapeze artists!

![Still from The Greatest Showman: a pink-haired trapeze artist flying through the air](/writing/vocus/the-greatest-showman/02.jpg)

## How can you not love how real it is

### How can you not love Zendaya

She really became a trapeze artist and did it herself. You can't help loving Zac Efron either, because he did it himself too. Both of them started out as kids in song-and-dance roles, and here they strike up a new kind of spark together. And above them both you have a genuine musical theater actor, Hugh Jackman. I never really followed Wolverine, so to me he'll always be the guy who sang and danced his way through hosting the Oscars (2009, <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Terhj8mjPwY>).

And of course, the remarkable performers in it are real!!! Doesn't it hit you the same way as hearing that Nolan was making *Oppenheimer* without visual effects and using a "real" atomic bomb? ([https://www.juksy.com/article/119264-《奧本海默》核爆場面來真的！導演親測原子彈試爆，不靠特效引網熱議！](https://www.juksy.com/article/119264-%E3%80%8A%E5%A5%A7%E6%9C%AC%E6%B5%B7%E9%BB%98%E3%80%8B%E6%A0%B8%E7%88%86%E5%A0%B4%E9%9D%A2%E4%BE%86%E7%9C%9F%E7%9A%84%EF%BC%81%E5%B0%8E%E6%BC%94%E8%A6%AA%E6%B8%AC%E5%8E%9F%E5%AD%90%E5%BD%88%E8%A9%A6%E7%88%86%EF%BC%8C%E4%B8%8D%E9%9D%A0%E7%89%B9%E6%95%88%E5%BC%95%E7%B6%B2%E7%86%B1%E8%AD%B0%EF%BC%81))

### When every song and every performance is the real thing

What's left not to appreciate? Listening to "Never Enough" (<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6jZVsr7q-tE>), it's hard not to cry.

### Zac + Zendaya

Then there's "Rewrite the Stars" (<https://youtu.be/yO28Z5_Eyls>), where the two Zs, Zac and Zendaya, finally break free of the veterans around them and get to shine. Speaking for myself, you really will fall for both of them.

Zac Efron got his commercial success starring in *High School Musical*, and Zendaya was one of the leads in *Shake It Up*. You could call that deliberate casting, but that deliberate choice is exactly what made this the most beautiful image in the film.

[▶ YouTube](https://youtu.be/yO28Z5_Eyls)

Zendaya is also the member of this cast with more than 100 million Instagram followers. That said, the scene where she waits by the hospital bed for Zac to wake up is shot from that brutal low angle nobody looks good from, and I thought it was brilliant.

### This Is Me

Not to mention the film's signature song, "This Is Me" (<https://youtu.be/5_WzSbi8aR8>), which belongs on any top-ten list of songs you have to learn, hear and love in this lifetime. Every one of these songs, tossed off like it's nothing, is a masterpiece.

## How could happiness be boring?

One scene I love comes after P. T. Barnum's theater is burned down, when the critic sits down with Barnum for a heart-to-heart. He says he'll never accept the circus as art, but that putting people of every color and class on the same stage together is something another critic might call a celebration of humanity.

The way P. T. Barnum's circus is received in the film is a lot like real life (see the reference image): the public loves it, and the critics aren't buying it. Well, I'd just like to say, they probably haven't been truly happy in a long time!

I opened this piece with "Since when has happiness ever been boring?" It's from the opening stretch of the film, where Charity pushes back while talking with the kids: how could happiness ever be boring?

### Her happiness is P. T. Barnum himself

For Charity, happiness is P. T. Barnum himself, the home they built, and the love at the heart of that home. That love shows up in the Wishing Machine the grown-up Barnum builds, and in the lantern he brought for her to play with when they were kids (from 1:32, <https://youtu.be/fCElqWdviUI?t=92>).

### A textbook American dream

For P. T. Barnum, though, life is one long scheme to climb out of his class, and not a little, all the way. If you look at the real P. T. Barnum's life, he did succeed to some degree, but he walked over a lot of people's blood to get there. In the film, he tries every angle he can think of: making money and joining the propertied class, then bringing in Phillip Carlyle as his way into high society, which leads him to Jenny Lind, whom he brings to America on a tour that's a huge success. A textbook American dream.

In this couple, we see that Charity is living off a happy childhood that holds her up her whole life, while P. T. Barnum has to spend his whole life buying his childhood back. Luckily the film doesn't need to dig much deeper there, or it would lose its wonderful comedic tone.

### A benchmark film for film literacy

*The Greatest Showman* is a great benchmark film for teaching film literacy. It's entertaining and educational at the same time, and you can feel the pulse of American history in it. The story gives a full, positive account of P. T. Barnum's life without hiding his worse side. The director knows music video camerawork and visual symbols inside out, and he gives this modern film the complete storytelling of a classic. It's really hard to find fault with it.

Worth watching again and again.

In the film, everyone reaches their happiness, but in real life we're still struggling. How can you not love movies? They take you into a dream and then make it real, and for these 120 minutes you get to soar around inside the world of the film.
Movies always end, and sooner or later we all have to face the challenges of real life. While you were in there, did you take a moment to have a real talk with yourself?

Happiness, boring? Getting there is a thrill!
