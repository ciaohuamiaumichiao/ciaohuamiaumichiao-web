## Matilda

Roald Dahl's classic children's book *Matilda* is the story of a five-year-old girl who loves to read and is extraordinarily clever. Her father is a dishonest used-car dealer, and her mother is a vain woman who buys right into male chauvinism and cares about her looks more than anything else in the world!

![Cover of Roald Dahl's Matilda, a girl standing on a pile of books](/writing/vocus/trunchbull-and-matilda/01.jpg)

Even at five, Matilda is hungry to read. Almost by chance she finds comfort in books, and makes friends with the kind librarian, Mrs. Phelps. Inside those books, Matilda finds her own abilities. With everything she has soaked up from all that reading, once she starts primary school her teacher, Miss Honey, quickly notices her gift and tries to persuade the headmistress, Miss Trunchbull, to give her special lessons.

That's where the story starts heading into a world an ordinary five-year-old probably couldn't make sense of. Miss Trunchbull is a former Olympic hammer thrower, and she really will grab a child and hurl them out of the school grounds like a hammer. From the older kids, Matilda learns that the headmistress has a cupboard for punishing students. It's pitch dark inside, and there's broken glass and nails. Get caught making mischief and you're locked in there all day, standing, not allowed to lean or sit, and you usually come out aching all over!

![Still from Roald Dahl's Matilda the Musical, Miss Trunchbull yelling at Matilda](/writing/vocus/trunchbull-and-matilda/02.jpg)

In the book, Matilda discovers she has a superpower she can control with her mind. Through all the unfair treatment, she uses her imagination and her powers to fight back against Miss Trunchbull's bizarre ideas, gets justice for the bullied Miss Honey, and ends up back in an ideal life!

The book has been adapted into films and a stage musical, and on Netflix you can watch both the 1996 *Matilda* and the 2022 *Roald Dahl's Matilda the Musical*.

![Matilda and Roald Dahl's Matilda the Musical on Netflix](/writing/vocus/trunchbull-and-matilda/03.jpg)

## A high school junior's life, lost

In real life, though, the ending might not be as pretty as a children's book. At a high school in central Taiwan, the director of student affairs caught some students vaping, and one of them claimed the e-cigarette had come from a junior in 11th grade. The junior got a minor demerit. Later, while carrying a classmate's jacket, he ran into the director, and a military instructor stopped him to search him. The classmate's jacket had a vape in the pocket. Another minor demerit.

From then on, the director searched him often, made things hard for him, and handed him a warning every chance he got. If someone was late, if an outsider got onto campus, if a classmate lost money, the director and the instructor decided he was behind it. No evidence needed. It was a real-life version of the old Chinese fable about the man who loses his axe and becomes sure the neighbor's boy stole it, so everything the boy does looks like a thief's. They even threatened him with juvenile court. "I'll keep asking until you admit it," the instructor told him.

He bought stacks of the white envelopes people use for funeral gifts and scattered them all over his classroom and his floor of the building, announcing that he was going to leave. This March, he took his own life at home. At the morning assembly, the school said only that it had been an accident.

## Standing with them against bullying

Every time I read *Matilda* or watch one of the films, the unequal bullying in it makes me angry. It's different from being bullied by kids your own age. This kind comes with the threat of power behind it, and it leaves the person being bullied with no room to breathe. Thinking back on growing up, if you wanted to stay out of a bully's reach, you had to arm yourself and get strong. Swallowing it again and again, or keeping the peace on the surface, only grows a bigger kind of violence. I was never bullied, because I armed myself and let people know I wasn't someone they could bully.

Nien Ti-Shun, someone I look up to a lot, recommended a picture book that's published in Taiwan as *There's a Big Fox at School* (the French original is *Un renard dans mon école*). A little boy gets singled out by a fox at school, and it keeps picking on him. At first he ignores it, but the fox keeps getting bigger, until it turns into a wolf and then a tiger, and along the way it attacks him more than once. Then one day, after another round of bullying, he sees that even the kids who were just watching have turned into tigers and started hitting him too. So he tells his mother. She believes him, and she goes to the school to deal with it.

![Cover of the picture book There's a Big Fox at School](/writing/vocus/trunchbull-and-matilda/04.jpg)

I think the most effective way to face bullying isn't passive endurance, it's active resistance. That includes using the strength of teachers and parents. You have to find a way to face it and deal with it. And as parents, we can keep reminding our kids that we're behind them at home, full of love and strength, and that whatever hardship or unfairness they run into in life, they can come home to be healed and supported.

When I first posted this, I put the book Ti-Shun recommended and where to buy it in the comments. If you'd like to spend that time with your kids, or just encourage each other to be brave, try leaning on this picture book. While your kids still cling to you and beg for a bedtime story, keep them company, and give them a story they'll keep coming back to long after they've grown up!

![Inside There's a Big Fox at School, the boy surrounded by tigers](/writing/vocus/trunchbull-and-matilda/05.jpg)

## The Glory

I should probably close with the big show of spring 2023! Writer Kim Eun-sook hides her Easter eggs in the script itself. The way every character's personality and setup clash, and the way the plot unfolds, leaves endless things to discuss, and I think the internet already has more reviews and breakdowns of that than anyone could read.

![Korean Netflix character poster for The Glory](/writing/vocus/trunchbull-and-matilda/06.jpg)

But the show's core belief, that evil gets punished and good wins out, is probably the bit of sunlight more people are hoping to see. According to South Korean figures, reported cases of school bullying there jumped from 11,750 in 2013 to 23,673 in 2016, 31,240 in 2017, 32,632 in 2018 and 31,130 in 2019. More than half involved physical violence, and the numbers for verbal abuse and extortion kept climbing too.

And plenty of us who grew up in Taiwan's school system have, to some degree, been through bullying or seen it happen. Our reporting system may just be a bit slow. The case at that high school in central Taiwan was only reported after things had already gone this far.

Whether it's flaws in the system or gaps in the rules, there's a lot we need to improve and talk through. But what we can do right now is be there for our own kids, so they really know they're loved, and so they can face an unknown future with empathy and courage, together with us!
