That day in class I asked everyone: when you eat curry rice, do you mix it? "Of course you mix it." "You have to mix it first." Almost everyone raised a hand, and someone said braised pork rice is the same, you definitely mix it. I said okay, let me tell you something. Curry rice or braised pork rice, I don't mix either of them. I'd even say you absolutely must not mix it. Mixing it ruins it.

I'm from Tainan. You all say our braised pork rice is really sweet, but to us, that's called normal. I'll eat a piece of the pork first and feel exactly how fatty that fat is, whether the sauce is fragrant or salty, how salty, how sweet, and whether maybe they went a little heavy on the star anise. Then I'll have a bite of plain rice and see if today it's cooked wetter and stickier or a bit less sticky. And then I start playing. What does it taste like with more rice and less sauce? What about more sauce and less rice? By the time I've gone through that round, the bowl has pretty much collapsed anyway, and that's when I mix everything together. Oh, so this is what it's like mixed. One bowl, and I get five flavors out of it. Okay, I'm not saying this is the standard way to eat it. It's just my way.

It reminds me of *Gabai Granny* (佐賀的超級阿嬤), which was huge when I was a kid. The grandson says, Grandma, I want to do a sport, and Grandma says, then run, running is free. Grandma, I'm so hot. Then go run, you'll cool off once you're running. I've always loved that part. Creativity is everywhere. It's just a question of whether we dig for it, and whether we notice that we're feeling these things.

## Laid down one layer at a time

A chef presses a lot of things together so that you take one bite and it becomes a kind of harmony. But I make things, so I always want to take it apart and see what exactly went into it. That day the classroom next door happened to be Dawson's arranging class, and I thought it was amazing. Darn it, they should've just told us all to go over and listen.

Arranging is actually laid down track by track too. First the drums, then the bass, then guitar, guitar effects, piano. Then the bass feels off, so you add a second layer of bass, a third, a second keyboard, a third, then ambient sound. Only then do you start stacking vocals: one layer, two, three, high parts, low parts, harmonies, and at the very, very end, the lead vocal. I've heard that for one Mayday song, 恆星的恆心, Ashin sang it 18 times and they stacked all of them together, so it's really 18 Ashins singing. No wonder none of us can ever sound like Ashin. There are 18 of him in there!

That's seasoning, and it's also layering. Mille-feuille is the same: roll the pastry flat, fold it back, fold it back again, then bake it, and only then does it become the dessert you eat.

So next time you have braised pork rice, why not try a bite of just the meat first, then a bite of rice? There's still time to mix it at the end.
