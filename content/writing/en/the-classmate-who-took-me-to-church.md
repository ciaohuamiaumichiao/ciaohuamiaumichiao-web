I'm from Tainan, and I went to the best elementary school in Tainan. At least, that's what I've always believed. In fourth grade, a new kid who'd just come back from the US joined our class. He didn't have to write his essays in Chinese characters. He could just write them in English and have his mom translate them into Chinese at home. No wonder they were so good. Your mom was editing them for you! The teacher gave him special treatment, and on top of that he was handsome, nicely tanned, a fast runner, and great at school. He was good at everything, really everything. The only thing was, he was shorter than me.

In sixth grade we were in the same class again, and one day he suddenly asked me, "Hey, do you want to come to church?" I thought, ohhh, so that's how you got so good. You go to church. Otherwise, how could anyone at our school be better than me? So I said sure, I'll go. After that I started going to Bread of Life Church in Tainan, came to know God in junior high, and I've kept walking that road ever since. I'm really grateful to him. He's a genuinely, genuinely good guy.

We ended up at the same junior high too, but he was in the kind of class where everyone did especially well, and I was in the kind of class where everyone did especially badly. Then he got into Tainan First Senior High School and I went to Tainan Second. After that he got into the diplomacy department at National Chengchi University, double majored in political science, minored in economics, and played on the basketball and volleyball teams for all three departments. I still wonder where he found the extra hours. And me? I got into a somewhat odd school, Shih Hsin University, to study film. We were the last class to take the old joint college entrance exam, back when they still printed the results lists with the minimum admission score on them. I went to check, and the lowest score in my year was, well, me... Later he went to a great school in the US, and we each went our own way.

## I studied film, you know

After graduating I joined a church, and if you join Bread of Life Christian Church in Taipei, of course you serve. I sat in the congregation watching what the team was putting on the screens and thought, hey, I studied film. If I joined, this team would have a lot of room to improve. Okay, I'm in. I'm going to change the world! Then I joined and became the newest of the new, and of course I could do everything and shot better than anyone, so it's been smooth sailing straight to the top ever since. Okay, I'm kidding.

Later the church was opening a gospel center, and my wife and I said yes. They had no youth pastor there, so we went to be with the young people. For a while, Monday was band practice, Tuesday was rest, Wednesday was the gathering in Zhongshan, Thursday and Friday had gatherings too, and Saturday was a service at Bread of Life plus another service in Zhongshan. It was basically a pastor's life, and honestly it was a lot of fun. Outside church, I started two companies, both doing video. What I want to do is church work, nonprofit work, projects that help society. They probably pay less, but they mean more, so life is a bit lean. But look at me, I'm still eating well enough to stay chubby.

We have two kids. My son is in eighth grade now and my daughter is in sixth. Once they turn 18, things should get a bit easier, right? Sometimes I think, if he hadn't asked me that one question that day, I don't know where I'd be now. You probably have someone like that in your life too, don't you? One offhand invitation, and your life ends up on a completely different road.

Oh, right. By high school he was taller than me. So now, he really is better than me at everything.
