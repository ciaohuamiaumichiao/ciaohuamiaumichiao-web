## New research from Yu Takagi and colleagues at Osaka University: scan the brain with fMRI, and you can reconstruct the images we see

![First page of Yu Takagi's paper, comparing images reconstructed from fMRI signals](/writing/vocus/black-mirror-crocodile/01.jpg)

[Read the paper](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.11.18.517004v2.full.pdf)

This has been all over my feed for almost three days, and it got me thinking that the future I imagined as a kid might actually start taking shape before I close my eyes for good.

The basic idea is to turn the electrical signals in the brain into images using fMRI. Subjects first look at and memorize specific images. During the scan they're asked to recall them, the equipment captures the signals, and it tries to draw what they saw. In several sets of results, you can see the output looks clearly similar to the originals.

In other words, as a director, I won't have to draw storyboards by hand anymore!? (Kidding.)

## The memory reader: Black Mirror, "Crocodile"

As a film fan, when something this close to science fiction shows up, then besides the anime *Top Secret: The Revelation* (*Himitsu*) that everyone keeps recommending, of course I have to share where this technology might be in ten years. And that's *Black Mirror*, Charlie Brooker's series, Season 4, Episode 3: "Crocodile."

![Promotional poster for Black Mirror "Crocodile," a close-up of the lead actress](/writing/vocus/black-mirror-crocodile/02.jpg)

The wide-eyed heroine has a successful career and a happy family, and she refuses to pay for a mistake she made when she was young. She was only an accomplice, but she won't let her ex-boyfriend write a confession letter and ruin everything. One after another, she kills her ex-boyfriend and then an insurance investigator's family of three. Four people. Even laying out the plot that bluntly doesn't spoil a thing.

Ever since Season 2, I've watched *Black Mirror* a bit like *Doraemon*. Every episode seems to pull out some little gadget that turns the whole story upside down until it's completely out of control. In this episode, the gadget is a small computer. The person being questioned wears a device that shows the images in their head in real time. You could say it's like an ultrasound machine for memory, except instead of imaging a baby, it images brainwave signals. And the art department clearly knew what they were doing: this memory reader looks completely convincing.

But how does the story use the device? Who would agree to put it on? Here the plotting is genuinely persuasive. The memory reader works as a kind of evidence tool. In legal cases it can help investigators judge evidence, and before things get that far, insurance companies are allowed to use it as part of the evidence-gathering they need for claims. It's a really well-thought-out design. With a reader like that, you could almost create a society with no lies, because nobody can lie!?

The device and the plot hook into each other and spiral into a mess nobody can clean up. And in the end, what you have to ask yourself comes back to the big question inside your own heart, and to the punishment of the law in the real world.

![Still from Black Mirror "Crocodile," a woman with a suitcase walking toward snowy mountains](/writing/vocus/black-mirror-crocodile/03.jpg)

The episode looks like it was shot somewhere near the Arctic Circle, and the cold compositions, colors and atmosphere stretch the episode's theme even wider. Naming the episode after a crocodile seems meant to teach the audience something, too.

A lot of reviews dig into the crocodile, with explanations like these:

The crocodile principle: say a crocodile bites your foot. If you try to use your hands to pull your foot free, the crocodile bites your foot and your hand. The more you struggle, the more of you it gets. So if a crocodile has your foot, your only chance is to give up the foot. The moment you know you've made a mistake, get out right away. No excuses, no hoping, no praying, no other moves. Don't try to adjust your position, hedge or do anything else pointless. Take the loss and get out!

Crocodile tears: people used to believe crocodiles shed fake tears while eating their prey. In fact, especially when they've been out of the water too long and their eyes dry out, their tear glands produce tears to keep their eyes moist, which makes them look like they're crying. Here it points to how cold the heroine is about feelings when her own interests are at stake.

Whichever meaning you go with, for me personally this episode wasn't quite as gripping, but we're talking about the difference between a 5 and a 4.8. I still have endless respect for *Black Mirror*! If you'd like a feel for what a prototype of this new fMRI memory reading might look like, I recommend *Black Mirror*, Season 4, Episode 3.
