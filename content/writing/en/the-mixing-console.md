It was September of my first year of junior high, in Tainan. The church worship band was performing and needed someone on the mixing console. My classmate Steven and I were both serving at church back then, and nobody else there knew how to run it, so I stepped up.

It was the first time I'd ever touched sound gear. I stood alone in front of the mixer, and I wasn't really sure what any of the buttons, faders or EQ knobs were for, so I just listened and tried things at random. But I remember that afternoon so clearly. I was lit up. Not lit up like someone on stage under the lights. Lit up the way you are standing at the sound desk down on the floor.

## Steven, who took me to church

I met Steven in fourth grade. He'd just come back from the US and couldn't write a single Chinese character, but he was still the best in the class at every subject. A year later, his Chinese handwriting was nicer than anyone's. Isn't that ridiculous! I loved tagging along with him. In sixth grade we were in the same class again, and one day he asked if I'd ever been to church. I said no. He said, then come. So I went, and I haven't left in 30 years.

When it was time to pick a junior high, he'd grown up in the US and didn't know anything about the schools in Tainan, so I told him, at Chien Hsing Junior High you don't have to cut your hair, go there! He said okay, and we both changed our household registrations to get into that school district and ended up at Chien Hsing together. Later he got into Tainan First Senior High and I went to Tainan Second. After that he went to the US for his PhD and he's still there, and I kept doing my video work. And now, he's my son's godfather.

I often feel that besides Jesus, he also introduced me to myself.

## So this is backstage

Back to that mixing console. I still can't quite explain what was going through my head in that moment. I think I suddenly realized there's a place where you don't have to stand on stage or be under the lights, but what you do makes the people on stage shine, and lets the people in the audience settle down and follow the melody into some kind of feeling. We'd been busy in the back for hours, and then a whole room of people got to really enjoy that time. The sentence that popped into my 13-year-old head was: "Oh, so this is backstage."

Of course I didn't know it then, but pretty much everything I've done in the 30 years since grew out of that moment. Running sound at church, stage lighting as a student, side jobs filming for people, then into the industry shooting music videos, running concert production, shooting brand commercials, and now curating exhibitions and working as a brand storytelling consultant. Every one of those looks a little different, but I seem to have always loved standing at the back, watching the people up front shine.

People ask me, after all this time, why don't I step out front myself? I've been on camera in music videos, I could be a lead singer, right? Hey, it's not that I'm shy. In high school I was in the camp counselor club, and I can talk for an hour, no problem. My friends even complain that I'm too blunt and kind of a jerk. What I want are those moments when 1,000 people down there are singing, crying and talking to God. Live, it's all of us building that atmosphere together. In a music video, it's the whole team working to drop the audience into that world. They'll remember that day they met God. They don't need to remember us. Really, they don't.

## Stepping forward, just a little

The exhibition was called "Backstage is a WORLD," and I gave it a Chinese name that means something like "a gathering place for backstage people."

I've been backstage for 30 years, and only in the last few did I realize that if the people backstage never speak up, the things that have happened in this industry, what we've built up, the things other people taught me, might just disappear along with us. So I've started stepping forward a little: writing, putting on exhibitions, making my own videos. I'm not trying to become a front-of-stage person. I just want the backstage spot to be seen a little more. If you work backstage too, part of that exhibition and part of these pieces were written for you.

The wall at the exhibition said "for the light," with "for family" and "for myself" next to it, along with the things I believe. That's the version I could see at 41. In five years it might change, and five years after that it might change again. But that 13-year-old kid pushing faders at random behind the mixing console is still here.
