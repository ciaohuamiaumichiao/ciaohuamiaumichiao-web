I have a friend who barely ever posts Instagram stories. Then one day he suddenly posted one on a dark gray background with just a few words: "Things have been a little hard lately." Fewer than 100 people saw it. Two days later, I got a call from another mutual friend: "Did you see that story from so-and-so? Did he get divorced?"

That honestly gave me a shock. Those two are something like five or six social circles apart, with maybe a dozen people they both know. But one black story with a line of gray text had traveled five circles out in two days. He never wrote the word "divorce," not once. But that color, those few words, that heavy tone had already told everyone how he was doing.

## We all live under handles

These days we all actually live under code names. Open your contacts and look: a lot of people aren't saved under their real names at all. They're Tiger, Timothy, so-and-so's Instagram handle. When you think of someone, what comes to mind first is often their handle. That's not a bad thing. It just means your handle is your brand. Every picture you post, every line in your stories, every heart you tap is quietly telling people what kind of person that handle is.

If you post food every day, people assume you know your food, whether or not you're actually a foodie. Post gym check-ins for three months straight and everyone automatically files you under fitness person. One year I posted 30 screenshots from Joshua Band music videos in a row, each one captioned "love this so much." Yes, okay, that person is me. That year, in everyone's eyes, I was probably just a Joshua Band superfan...

## So how do you want to be seen?

None of this is right or wrong. I just think it's worth being a little aware that you're sending signals every day. Once in a while, look back at the stories you posted this week and string them together. Do they make people's picture of you clearer or blurrier? Looking back three months from now, will you think it was pretty good? If you suddenly had to look for a new collaboration tomorrow, would this Instagram help you out or hold you back?

So for the next seven days, I'd like to invite you to try a small exercise. Every time you're about to post a story, pause for a second and ask whether this one pushes you a little toward where you want to go, or pulls you a little further away. Keep posting your random stuff and your food, that's totally fine. Just go from not thinking about it at all to thinking about it. That's plenty.
