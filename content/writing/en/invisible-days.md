Rick, Timothy and I have spent the past ten years doing basically the same thing: making things that don't look good look good, making ordinary things look like gold, making people who aren't comfortable on camera look polished. We're really good at it, and honestly, it's how we pay the bills.

And now AI can do it too. Toss in any photo and it'll retouch it until it looks professional. Shoot a terrible video and it'll fix the lighting and the sound, and edit it for you while it's at it. So whatever we're competing on next, it's probably not who looks prettier anymore. That stopped being special a long time ago.

## The line where the bowl broke

Japan has a technique called kintsugi. When a bowl breaks, you mend the cracks with lacquer mixed with gold powder, and once it's repaired, that gold line stands out more than the whole bowl. Every time I see one, I feel like the beauty isn't really in the bowl. It's in the way that gold line has grown into it.

People seem to be like that too. We've all fallen, been sad, been hurt, and then spent a long, long time learning to live with those wounds. What that time leaves behind is very hard for anyone else to copy. AI can make something perfect, and it can make something that pretends to be imperfect, but it can't take away a stretch of road a person has actually walked.

Only in the last few years have I slowly come to understand that when you're willing to share a little of your vulnerability on social media, that's often the easiest way for people to find their way in. We've all seen far too many glossy photos. We scroll past them and forget.

## My son is reading classical poetry

My son has been reading Tang and Song dynasty poetry lately, like the Xin Qiji poem about being young and not yet knowing the taste of sorrow, loving to climb the tower, and forcing sorrow into new verses anyway. We've been going through the poets one by one, Li Bai, Du Fu, Su Dongpo, Xin Qiji, and looking at when each of them actually wrote. It turns out a lot of it was written while they were banished, while they were down and out, so sad they could barely write and yet with no choice but to keep writing. What came out of those times still hits hard when you read it today.

So while putting this exhibition together, I kept thinking: if every photo were shot on a Hasselblad, lit perfectly and composed with absurd precision, then what is the exhibition actually showing people? Everyone has already seen what perfect looks like, and plenty of people are making it. I'd rather show you my imperfect side.

At the talk I said that a brand story is something you dig up, not something you wrap up. The little things you do every day, the shows and books you're willing to spend time on, the article you saved halfway through scrolling on the toilet, the things you care about and will put real effort into: those are actually the most useful material you have.

When "Golden" was on stage at this year's Oscars, the lead singer said it was so moving because decades of struggle by Korean American immigrants had finally been seen. The song can talk about being done hiding and finally shining the way you were born to, because the person who wrote it really had been hidden. I think someone who had never been hidden probably couldn't write about shining like that at all.

## Technique is there to serve people with ideas

The three of us all freelance, and when you get down to it, what we sell is technique. We serve people who already know what they want. It sounds a little brutal to say out loud, but I've seen so many brand owners keep learning technique: how to shoot Reels, how to run ads, how to do SEO, how to use AI. They just never stop to think about what they actually want to do.

Now AI has dragged the bar for technique all the way down to the floor. Everyone can do it, and the only difference left is why you're doing it. AI can't help you with that one. Only you can answer it.

A lot of people hear this and say, I'd love to, but I honestly don't know who I am. That's okay. I'm 41 and I still don't know. What I care about now is whether I get to know myself a little better along the way. And a brand doesn't have to wait until you've figured everything out to get started. As you go, you might find the direction you assumed isn't quite right, so you turn a little, and grow out a bit further.

I could hold this exhibition at Ivy's space because we already knew each other and she trusts me. If I were a stranger who walked in asking to put on a show, she'd have no reason to say yes. That's probably what a personal brand is: letting what you've built up show, bit by bit. Show a little, build up a little more, and someday, when you want to do something beyond anything you've done before, someone who knows who you are now will be willing to give you a hand.
