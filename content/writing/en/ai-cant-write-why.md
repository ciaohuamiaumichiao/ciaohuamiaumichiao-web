Last month a longtime client messaged me, and he got straight to the point: "Hey Kimi, ChatGPT and Claude can both write scripts now. Do we even need you for the next ad? Can't I just prompt it myself?" I stared at that message for a good while and didn't answer right away. I wasn't offended. It's just that I'd been chewing on this exact question for months, and if I was going to answer, I wanted to think it through first.

## Honestly, AI writes pretty well

I'm not going to pretend otherwise. For the past three months, every project I've taken on, I've had Claude and ChatGPT draft a script skeleton first. What comes back is well structured, the pacing is reasonable, and now and then there's a line that makes me go "oh, nice." If what you need is something that looks like a script, then yes, it writes you a script. A small brand on a tight budget that just needs a usable video can probably get by with an AI-written one.

But one time I gave Claude a client's brief and asked for a brand film. It handed it in fast. I read it and asked, "Why are we making this?" It answered, very earnestly, that the purpose of the ad was to build brand image, increase consumer awareness and communicate the brand's core values.

I laughed out loud, because that answer works for any brand, any product, at any time. The films that actually land have a very narrow "why" behind them: a particular brand, at a particular moment, wanting to tell a particular group of people something only they'll get. AI isn't that brand, and it doesn't know those people.

## Most clients don't know yet either

I once wrote a piece about [the three questions I ask before taking a brand film](/en/writing/clients-dont-know-what-they-want). In it I said that at least eight out of ten brands we've worked with didn't know why they wanted to make the thing. Think about that. If the client doesn't know, where is the AI supposed to find out?

AI is best at taking a clear task and finishing it. Creative work almost never starts clear. Far more often, a client thinks they want A, would actually prefer B, and hasn't noticed. A big part of my job is sitting in that fog with them until we find it: watching where their expression changes, hearing which parts are industry talk and which parts they really mean, knowing what the brand has been through in the last three years, and quietly guessing whether their boss will buy it. A better prompt doesn't fix that. That room needs a person sitting in it.

So this is how I work now. The client's email goes to AI first to pull apart what they haven't said clearly. Treatment skeletons, shot lists, first drafts of case studies, briefs for collaborators, captions after the shoot: all that grunt work gets a first pass from AI. What I keep for myself is judgment. Whether to take the job, who the film is really for, what the brand most needs to say right now, every trade-off on set, which shot survives in the edit. I tell friends AI is like my assistant director. Incredibly useful, but it doesn't know the client, and it doesn't have the instincts I've ground out on set, a little at a time, over fifteen years.

## What I told him

I wrote back: "Good question, so I'll be straight with you. If the next one is a standard brand ad, an AI script might score 70 and mine might score 85. Whether those 15 points are worth my fee is your call. But if you want a film your customers remember for five years, AI doesn't have those five years of experience. Talk to me about which kind the next one is."

Three days later he replied: "Let's set up a time to talk."

At that point it was pretty clear to me. The clients who leave will mostly be the ones who think 70 is enough, and that's fair. For the ones who want 85 and up, AI actually lets me work faster and cheaper, so I can take on a few more people. So I'm really not that worried about AI writing scripts, copy or designs. It's going to do those things. What I care about is not handing over "why are we doing this" along with everything else. That's something you build day by day with your clients and with yourself, and AI hasn't lived those days. What about you? What would you hand to AI, and what would you keep in your own hands?
