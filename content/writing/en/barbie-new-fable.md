## Barbie is me!

![Margot Robbie in a pink gown on the cover of Vogue's Summer issue](/writing/vocus/barbie-new-fable/01.jpg)

Robbie stars in the Greta Gerwig-directed Barbie, due in theaters in July. Versace dress. Chopard Haute Joaillerie earring. Fashion Editor: Gabriella Karefa-Johnson.  
  
Photographed by Ethan James Green, Vogue, Summer 2023.

> *Barbie* is a 2023 American [romantic comedy](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B5%AA%E6%BC%AB%E5%96%9C%E5%89%A7) [musical](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%AD%8C%E8%88%9E%E7%89%87) film based on [Mattel](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%BE%8E%E6%B3%B0%E5%85%92)'s [Barbie doll](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%8A%AD%E6%AF%94%E5%A8%83%E5%A8%83), directed by [Greta Gerwig](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%91%9B%E8%8E%89%E5%A1%94%C2%B7%E8%91%9B%E8%96%87), who co-wrote the screenplay with [Noah Baumbach](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%AB%BE%E4%BA%9E%C2%B7%E6%B3%A2%E6%8B%9C%E5%85%8B). After a long run of [animated films](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%8A%AD%E6%AF%94%E7%B3%BB%E5%88%97%E9%9B%BB%E5%BD%B1) and series, it is the first live-action film based on the doll. It stars [Margot Robbie](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%91%AA%E6%A0%BC%C2%B7%E7%BE%85%E6%AF%94) and [Ryan Gosling](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%91%9E%E6%81%A9%C2%B7%E9%AB%98%E6%96%AF%E6%9E%97), with [Will Ferrell](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A8%81%E7%88%BE%C2%B7%E6%B3%95%E6%B4%9B), [America Ferrera](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%89%BE%E7%BE%8E%E8%8E%89%E5%8D%A1%C2%B7%E5%BC%97%E7%91%9E%E5%A8%9C), [Simu Liu](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%8A%89%E6%80%9D%E6%85%95_(%E6%BC%94%E5%93%A1)) and [Kate McKinnon](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%87%B1%E7%89%B9%C2%B7%E9%BA%A5%E9%87%91%E5%84%82) in supporting roles. The story follows "Stereotypical Barbie," who one day notices she is changing, meets the cast-aside "Weird Barbie," and travels with her boyfriend "Stereotypical Ken" to the real world to find out how to fix it. What each of them goes through in the real world leads them to question who they are and what their identity is.

## What if

What if. I'm just saying *if*.

What if Warner Bros. hadn't [driven Nolan away](https://www.hypesphere.com/news/18883) and been left with almost no cards to play this summer? Would a script like this have gotten such a big marketing budget? Would we have gotten the accidental "[Barbenheimer](https://twitter.com/SaulGonzalezCA/status/1682935805181067265?s=20)" effect, with the global box office going off like a chain reaction in an atomic bomb, and making *Mission: Impossible* actually look impossible?

And what if Ken had been played by Justin Bieber instead of Ryan Gosling? Would the whole audience have skewed younger and broader, or would there be a lot of things the film just couldn't say?

## A pink Alice in Wonderland

![Group photo on the Barbie set, the director and cast in pink jumpsuits](/writing/vocus/barbie-new-fable/02.jpg)

"Barbie" director Greta Gerwig says she was surprised by the conservative takedown of her feminist feature, but she hopes everyone who sees it can get some "relief." (Jaap Buitendijk / Warner Bros. Pictures)

The silliness might go beyond what I imagined. I'd say even the script is silly-level. Remember how in every interview, whenever Margot Robbie talked about making the film, she couldn't help mentioning how they were walking on thin ice with the script. Even after landing someone as gifted as Greta Gerwig to write it herself, they were still worried it wouldn't get past the "higher-ups" (at Warner and Mattel).

And sure enough, maybe annoying Warner and Mattel pushed Greta Gerwig a little too far? Because in this film, you see the "higher-ups" looking nakedly foolish in every corner of the Barbie movie. And even being foolish, they have to stay foolish from start to finish, all the way to the end of the film.

In *Alice in Wonderland* there's a rabbit hole that connects two worlds. When Barbie gets to the real world, it starts looking for its own question, "Who am I?", just like Alice.

Barbie cries, and so does Alice. Alice keeps growing bigger and smaller, and in the end she becomes tiny enough to float on her own tears into the fantasy world. I'll remember Barbie's tears. When they appeared on Margot Robbie's perfect face, it was like a new switch flipped on. Tears are the key into a world, and every tear is telling you who you are. Greta Gerwig has said she cries while directing, too. When it suddenly hit me that this film is a fable, I cried. And when Ken discovers his own worth, I remember he cried as well.

![The Cheshire Cat from the animated Alice in Wonderland](/writing/vocus/barbie-new-fable/03.jpg)

Still from *Alice in Wonderland*

> "If you don't know where you're going, it doesn't matter where you are now. Just keep walking, and you'll end up somewhere." (the Cheshire Cat)

The Cheshire Cat is Alice's guide to waking up. When Alice is stuck in confusion, it just tells her to keep walking. Barbie does the same thing, moving forward toward a dream it doesn't understand at all, even though the problem in front of it is miles away from any dream or meaning in its life. What it's chasing may be the very thing it has always wanted to escape. But in the middle of all that chaos, a character shows up in Barbie's world (Gloria, played by America Ferrera) and gives a speech that sounds like a rant. That speech straightens the whole film out from silliness, and from that moment things become harder to predict. Because of that speech, I'm switching Barbie's pronoun from "it" to "she," and showing you the film's outermost layer: "a feminist film?" It's what turns the whole thing from silliness into a fable.

In *Alice in Wonderland*, the question is "Who am I?", and the way to find the answer is through time, through obsession, through dreaming. In *Barbie*, the question is also "Who am I?", but the way to find out becomes feminism.

## Feminism or patriarchy / Barbenheimer

A lot of people are calling this a feminist film, but to me, "feminism" might just be the antidote for a real world that feels powerless. When women are free to be themselves, to simply be people, to chase their dreams and achievements and let their energy out, then this real, powerless world has a chance to move on to its next stage: finding meaning, and then living inside that meaning!

It's like the film itself. "Feminism" became the antidote for *Barbie*: it lifted the box office, fulfilled Mattel's movie ambitions, filled the Nolan-shaped hole at Warner, and made Margot Robbie breathtakingly beautiful.

Is *Oppenheimer* a patriarchal film? I can only laugh and say, well, that's just how that era was. Beyond that, I haven't done the two-films-in-one-day thing yet to feel the hot-and-cold shock of Barbenheimer. But how lucky am I to be alive right now, watching Warner lose Nolan and then try to win him back, and watching Margot Robbie pull every resource together to turn *Barbie* into a phenomenon and a classic.

This summer, we all get to have a foot in history: Nolan's historical biopic *Oppenheimer*, built to last, and Margot Robbie's fairy-tale fable *Barbie*. One summer with two blockbusters that will go down in film history. What are you waiting for? Go watch them!
