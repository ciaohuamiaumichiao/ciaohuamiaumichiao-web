In college I studied in the Department of Radio, Television and Film at Shih Hsin University, on the film track. The department was split into three tracks, radio, TV and film, and everyone liked to say we were all one big family. We were not. We never took a single radio or TV class. The class I remember most from those years was called Acting, taught by Hsu Hua-Chien. He was a very young teacher back then. Now, well, he's gotten old too.

In one class he had all of us lie down on the stage, and then he said, "Now feel your body." I was thinking, what exactly is a college student supposed to feel this early in the morning? The only thing I could feel was: could class please not start so early? Then he said, first, notice whether the muscles in your right arm are twitching. What is this! Next, feel the sound of the air conditioning. Of course none of us got it back then, and Acting came and went just like that.

Later, as I grew up, in some very lonely moments, times when I was on my own, that class would suddenly pop back up. I started to feel that there are so, so many things in this world you can feel, and yet we rarely truly open our eyes, ears, nose and mouth. When you walk into a new space, how do you get to know it? When you come across something new, or old, or ancient, or something you once had, how do you want to interact with it?

## How close are you to the thing?

My job title out in the world is director, and that means I have to know how to do everything: camera, editing, lighting, all of it. I'm different from the senior colleague I teach this class with, who came up inside the TV station system. I knew from the day I graduated that I'd never work at a TV station, so I've always freelanced. When a job came in and I didn't know how to do it, I learned. When I didn't know whether I could pull it off, I said I'd give it a try first. Looking back over these ten-plus years, whether the things I made were any good often came down to how close I was to the thing. The ones I kept at a distance didn't turn out as well, and audiences can always tell.

Food is the same. When the chef really puts their heart into it, you're happy to pay an extra NT$200. Part of what you're buying at a chef's counter is their care, their performance, those few minutes they spend standing in front of you. I used to go to a stone-pot hotpot place where they always stir-fry right in front of you, and when they do, flames shoot up huge and the server yells, "Careful!" I think that moment alone was worth a hundred NT dollars. A lot of the time, when I make a film or run an event, that's what I'm really selling too: an experience.

## About words

That day, after eating some baguette, everyone had to write down five words, and a lot of people got stuck. Tasty, not tasty, hungry, and that was it. Can you cheat? Sure. If you can't say it, dance it for me, or draw it, that works too. But from my own experience, I still think you need words. You can take a word apart and think about it. Take the Chinese word for a cell phone, 手機, which breaks into "hand" and "machine." From "hand" you can keep going to the bones of the hand, the palm. So why is it called that and not something else? English has roots, words have origins, and Chinese, English and your own mother tongue all have so much hidden inside them that you can turn over again and again.

What creative people fear most is running out of inspiration. So where does inspiration come from? Probably from the places we don't know and haven't met yet. I always say knowledge is power, and ignorance is unlimited power. No, I'm kidding. What I mean is, go touch the things that make you uncomfortable, the things you don't know well. If you usually paint with your right hand, try painting with your toes. Okay, I'm just making things up now. But in the middle of that process, something new often shows up.

So next time you walk into a new space, maybe don't rush to take a picture with your phone. Just listen to the air conditioning for a bit. Think of it as finally making up Hsu Hua-Chien's early-morning class, many years late.
