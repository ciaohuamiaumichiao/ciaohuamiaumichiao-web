## Some films, you only need to see once

I don't necessarily mean that as a knock. Some films… you only need to see once. For me that's especially true of this kind: parents and kids, illness, goodbyes. For any family with children, it's a guaranteed tear-duct explosion at the box office. Every single time, you walk in with single eyelids and walk out with double ones, puffy from crying.

![BIG film poster, kids from the ward laughing among cloud illustrations](/writing/vocus/big-keep-films-alive/01.jpg)

Some friends' family really wanted to see it, and we were worried it was about to leave theaters, so we squeezed it in on Taiwan's election day. I'd been hearing for a long time that director Wei Te-sheng was preparing a film about childhood cancer, and I finally got to see it for myself.

*BIG* portrays six families, plus the doctors and other supporting roles it needs, and handles the relationships and personalities of nearly 20 characters at once. That gives us a way of watching it where we can just enjoy how director Wei handles the script. Looked at only from that angle, the way the character relationships are handled is textbook-level.

## Six families, one small slice of time in Ward 816

Six families go through a small slice of time together in Ward 816. Some kids lose their fight with cancer, some recover and go home, some are still in the middle of it, and some are learning to live alongside the illness. Each child has their own innocence and their own way of facing fear. The parents, with their different faiths, life situations and finances, turn the ward into a cross-section of society. Sometimes you frown, sometimes you laugh. And the animated interludes drawn especially to show the main character's inner world almost feel like they're warming the audience up for the re-release of *Princess Mononoke*, a reminder of that miraculous girl.

## Film as something handmade

You can see the film's ambition. Besides caring for sick children, it also offers warm tears as a wish for Christmas 2023. The Wei Te-sheng style of releasing a film and getting the funding in place shows what else a film can look like. After director Wei started a new wave in how Taiwanese films reach audiences, these recent marketing moves have also proven that a film can go around the industrial production pipeline and exist in the market more like a handmade work of art.

You'll notice a lot of sponsor placements in the film, and partner venues where scenes were shot. The team's deliberate approach isn't clumsy. It's meant to open up more ways for the film and real life to work together! Whether it's people visiting the locations or buying products to show support, linking the work to real things in the world means talking about a film is no longer limited to theaters and reviews, or to entertainment and date nights.

This approach also gives Taiwanese films the energy to reach a wider public and new audiences, expanding Taiwan's moviegoing population once again, so local films don't have to settle for certain genres and can create more possibilities!

More than 600 private screenings with talks, explaining and sharing the film's ideas with audiences one by one, taking photos and answering comments one step at a time. That look of fighting hard to stay alive: isn't director Wei fighting just as hard as Yuan-Yuan does in the film?

## Script vs. performance

The cast passes on the glow of big-name stars, and the casting is very precise overall. Pets Tseng caught my eye the most. Her performance is sincere and not at all forced, she knows exactly when to hold back and when to let go, and in how she delivers the dialogue in context, I think she found the most perfect balance between the character and who she really is.

That said, while the character relationships in the script are textbook-level, maybe because it has to handle so many people, if an actor in the moment isn't deep enough into the role and doesn't have time to rework the dialogue so it fits, a lot of the performance ends up forced and awkward. Especially the lines. On the page they might read fine, but when they're actually performed, there are plenty of moments where the delivery is unbearably awkward.

Wang Meng-lin's performance had us laughing our heads off, but he never gets out from under the "Wang Meng-lin" icon, and in every scene he's in, I kept getting pulled out of the story. That isn't really a flaw, though. It's a case worth discussing when it comes to dialogue, context and the actor's own persona. After all, later in the film, when he picks up his instrument, the character just exists, completely natural, without a single false note.

## Wei Te-sheng makes a film more than a film

Preparing, shooting and releasing a film doesn't happen so it can be reviewed. There may be plenty of thoughts about the technique and production in it, and director Wei always gives those of us in the industry something different to talk about and think through.

But the emotional weight the film carries is not something you can just brush aside. It could send even people with dry eyes out of the theater in tears, and a film that's guaranteed to set the tears flowing doesn't just appear on the market whenever you want one. On top of that, with the art direction and the marketing, Wei Te-sheng makes a film more than a film. It can be a comforting Christmas gift for 2023, and it can be a warm blessing planted in your life to recharge you. What a film can carry: he, Wei Te-sheng, really has shown us more possibilities!

So how could you not love movies?
