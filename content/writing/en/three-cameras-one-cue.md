On February 28, XEK put on a worship concert at the music venue downstairs from the CTS building in Taipei. Close to 400 people showed up, and by my rough count maybe ten of them were over 40. I was in the middle of the crowd with a camera thinking, wow, so this is what worship looks like for young people, and I was thrilled. I also wanted to ask what exactly our churches have been doing all this time... okay, no, never mind.

I originally quoted a fully directed setup: three cameras, a big monitor at the back, headsets on every operator. Camera 1 covers that side, camera 2 this side, camera 3 over here, and I switch live from the back. That was the theory. They said it was too expensive, which I understood. It's gospel music, it's an indie scene, there's no money. Fine. We changed it to three cameras shooting independently, and I'd cut it all together afterwards.

I very confidently promised two finished videos a week. Then the edit slowly drained me. Halfway through, I left to walk 348 km with Truth Church and House of Dream, and while I was walking he called: "Kimi, any chance you have time to edit?" I said, I'm walking. So February 28 turned into whatever month it is now. I've honestly lost track.

## The one thing I said before we rolled

The three camera operators that night were me, someone from my team, and a friend from another church who doesn't shoot for a living. The headsets had no audio. There was nothing. It was an extremely cheap production. So before we started I told them: we're only doing one thing tonight. We're in there worshipping, we just happen to be holding cameras. Shoot whatever you feel like shooting, leave one camera on a safe wide, and leave the rest to God.

My thinking was, the people on stage are already going wild, so why should we be shooting everything neat and level? Let's go a little wild too and see what happens. What I got back was a lot of bumping, swinging footage, and I spent a very long time in post digging through it.

That's actually the instruction I give most often when I direct at church. If I see your shot sitting perfectly level and polite, I'll call you down and say, let's refocus for a second. Do you know what worship is? If you feel like kneeling right now, kneel. Nobody can carry the weight of God, and if He knocks you down, go down. Everyone in this industry knows cameras are expensive, so expensive they start to feel more important than your life. But what's more important than your life is God. So if we ever work together, please, please, treat God as just slightly more important than the camera in your hands.

## The book

One book shaped me a lot: Tommy Tenney's *God's Favorite House*. Look it up if you're curious. His point is that what we're called to do isn't rebuild the Temple, it's rebuild the fallen tabernacle of David. The Temple was surrounded by walls. David's tabernacle had no walls. It was just a group of people singing, dancing and playing music, honestly and simply.

That book keeps sending me back to the same question: what am I actually chasing? A home in the expensive part of Taipei, or a happy family? A bigger church, or people who are genuinely connected? A team that runs like clockwork so I can tell the pastor we're doing great whenever he asks, or a team that actually knows what it's serving? If you don't feel like serving right now, that's fine. Rest first. God will call you back. And if we're short of people? It's fine. That's what full-time staff are for.

## The AI edit and my edit

In class that day I showed two versions of the same song. After the first one, people said it was pretty good, a few shots a bit shaky. I told them the whole thing was cut by AI, and that mine was better and I'd show them in a minute.

Mine jumps around more. The AI cut, and the kind of live directing we're all used to, lets you sit back and enjoy the view. I'd rather pull you inside. People who make media have to keep asking ourselves: are we showing you this, guiding you through it, keeping you company while you watch it, or putting you so far inside that you're not really watching anymore? Each of those is a completely different approach, and none of them is wrong. I told the dancers and painters in the class they can ask themselves the same question. What about you? Where do you want the people looking at your work to be standing?
