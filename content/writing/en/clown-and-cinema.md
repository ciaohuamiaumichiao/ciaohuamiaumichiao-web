I'm still soaking in the afterglow of the circus festival. After heading back north, work sent me on another quick dash down to Kaohsiung and Tainan: the same familiar warmth, the same cloudless sky.

The circus has one classic character: the clown. A clown usually wears a red nose, almost like a registered trademark.

![Collage of four people wearing red clown noses](/writing/vocus/clown-and-cinema/01.jpg)

Clowns always bring me back to Hou Hsiao-hsien's *The Sandwich Man* (兒子的大玩偶). This anthology is also seen as the beginning of the Taiwan New Wave. A core idea of the New Wave was realism, and this anthology film realistically portrays Taiwanese society at the time: the gap between rich and poor, the divide between city and countryside, the unfairness built into society. There are no answers in it, only flavors for us to slowly taste.

![Still from The Sandwich Man, a man in clown makeup holding a baby](/writing/vocus/clown-and-cinema/02.jpg)

My favorite thing is the short line printed on the film's poster: bring laughter to his son, keep the tears for himself. The clown seems to be born carrying this contradictory, complicated mission. Pulling off one show that gets a laugh, with adrenaline on your side, is no problem at all. But ten shows, or a hundred, with the same jokes and the same burst of energy... Maybe life is like that too. There are always times when you're wandering around at rock bottom, or when you have to keep digging deeper, the kind where you're about to strike oil.

Life is a tragedy when seen in close-up, but a comedy in long-shot. - Charlie Chaplin

There's no chicken soup for the soul here, just a late-night bowl with a bit of miso in it to warm your stomach. Be a Clown, We have nothing to lose.

At the time, of course, I had to recommend the 4K restoration of *A City of Sadness* by Hou Hsiao-hsien, a director who does things properly, the Hou way. It was back on the big screen, and anyone who hadn't seen it yet could get a ticket at [in89 Cinemax](https://www.facebook.com/in89in89).

Beyond the really comfortable viewing experience, what mattered more to me was that I could sit in the same theater with the teacher who always used to show me films in super blurry VCD quality. In my universe he's no longer here, but in some parallel universe, who knows, he's grinning from ear to ear watching *A City of Sadness* at more than eight times VCD quality, in this very same hall.

To Pa-yeh.

Feel free to share your memories of Professor Tseng Hsi-pa. Back then I also asked if anyone wanted to come see *A City of Sadness* with me... or else I'd very sadly go by myself, to see the sad *A City of Sadness*, with a sad heart.
