If I were running Taiwan's Indigenous Peoples Cultural Foundation, I'd want to ask: could some of the budget go into *Moana 2*? The reason is simple. If you want to help the general public get to know the Austronesian peoples, this film's potential reach is far beyond what we imagine!

*Moana* depicts Polynesian culture, and according to archaeological findings, Polynesian culture as a whole most likely traces its origins back to Taiwan. So for someone like me, who has spent years making content around Taiwan's Indigenous cultures, the island life and the pulse of the music in the film felt instinctively familiar and struck a deep chord.

On a weekend afternoon in early December, after finishing my morning plans, I drove straight to my favorite cinema, in89 Cinemax, settled into the LUXE hall, and joined the *Moana 2* "chee-hoo" crowd.

The huge auditorium had about 20 people in it. This time in89 put the film in its biggest hall, so our family of film nerds got to enjoy the best screen in Taipei. The LUXE hall strikes a great balance between picture and sound. The high-gain screen pushes up the brightness, and compared with the screens I'm used to, it made this riot-of-color animated film really pop. What you truly have to experience for yourself, though, are the seats in in89's Hall 1. Don't miss them. They're the most comfortable seats I've ever sat in at any cinema. And thanks to the special floor design in Hall 1, even in the third row from the front you can practically recline while you watch, and it still beats the front three rows of any other theater!

![The red seats and big screen of in89's Hall 1, the LUXE auditorium](/writing/vocus/moana-2-austronesian/01.jpg)

*Moana 2* is set three years after [Te Fiti's heart was returned](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E6%B5%B7%E6%B4%8B%E5%A5%87%E7%BC%98). Moana now has a little sister, Simea. Having become a wayfinder, Moana is called by her ancestors, puts together her own crew and sets sail again. She reunites with Maui, and together they cross Oceania into unknown waters to break an ancient curse and reconnect the islands of the sea!

Overall, the crew mostly opens up more possibilities and keeps the plot moving. For me it was still the Moana and Maui show. That said, the two of them together are absolutely worth the ticket on their own!

Visually, beyond Disney's usual riot of color, the director is himself Samoan, so the Austronesian feel comes through even more authentically. The editing between shots and the layered mix of Austronesian elements and American humor let the film shake off any sense of a culture lecture or a lesson, and yet by the end it builds an even stronger sense of the culture and of belonging!

![Moana 2 poster, Moana and Maui standing on a boat as they set sail](/writing/vocus/moana-2-austronesian/02.jpg)

That's why I said at the start: if I were the foundation, could I put some funding toward marketing around this film? I've always admired tattoos, but I still haven't been able to commit to getting one, because I don't really understand the culture. Then *Moana 2* helped me understand the heart of tattoo culture. Besides jumping on the bandwagon and wanting a tattoo of my own, I came away understanding what a tattoo means for a warrior! Maybe it's like Lin Yu-min on Taiwan's team at the 2024 Premier12: he's Amis, and the tattoos on his arm are his warrior's mark.

![A Taiwan pitcher in a blue No. 45 jersey, tattoos on his arm, mid-delivery](/writing/vocus/moana-2-austronesian/03.jpg)

In the Mandarin version you get a terrific lineup of Indigenous voice actors, and in the English version you get Dwayne "The Rock" Johnson being funny. Each has its own feel, and either one was absolutely worth seeing in a theater. Ideally at [in89](https://www.in89cinemax.com/index.aspx?TheaterId=3)!
