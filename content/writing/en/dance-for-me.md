If you can dance, the first thing grown-ups usually say is, "Come on, dance for me." If you can draw, it's "Come on, draw something for me." Grown-ups, please listen to what you're saying. My talent isn't there to put on a show for you. Okay, and say I actually do dance for you today. Are you with me? I mean really with me. Are you on the same wavelength as my dance, my painting, even a little? Is there any resonance at all?

Music is a bit different. Music is loud, the vibration is obvious, and everyone feels it the moment they hear it. But those of us who dance, paint or hold a camera don't have a sound. That day one of the dancers in class told us about a time they were completely caught up in worship and felt like someone nearby was filming, and then it turned out they weren't in the footage at all. The worship was real, but the picture didn't exist. I said, please, we in media have it way worse. This year the media track only got two students. We couldn't even run the class...

## We're the neck

There's a saying that the husband is the head and the wife is the neck. I think people who do media are that neck. The stage is the head, but where everyone ends up looking is where we turn it. You just can't see us doing it. In a way, we represent what this church is saying. The pastor preaches a sermon, and we amplify it a hundred times, a thousand times, ten thousand times, and play it for the world. So honestly, when a church sends people to learn media, of course they need to learn a way of thinking that's close to their own church. That puts us a little bit further from making art, because there's a lot more we have to take into account.

Speaking of amplifying, the person who's best at setting the atmosphere is actually the pastor. He's talking along, suddenly pauses for two seconds, and starts speaking with a catch in his voice, and you know you're about to get to the moving part. Another two-second pause, and suddenly he's loud and the whole room is fired up. I think we worshippers are the same. God chooses us to be the ones who change the atmosphere, in a service, in our lives, in the places we spend our time. A lot of people think worship is just there to bring peace and bless everyone, as if it only has one atmosphere. But worship actually has many atmospheres. Some are really joyful, and some are very quiet.

## "Is it done yet?"

This year I took on a client from outside our church circle, the not-very-considerate kind. That day I shot with him from 8 in the morning until 10 at night. In theory we should have gone to bed at the same time, right? Then first thing the next morning he messaged me: "Is it done yet?" He thought once the shoot was over, it was done. But our work starts after the shoot. Or really, it starts before the shoot: planning, shooting, post-production, then publishing it and doing the social media, and once we've gone all the way around, we come back and think about how to make the next one.

I'd guess it's the same for those of you who dance and paint. It's not that there's a hole in today's service and you just come fill it with a quick dance. We also have to receive, to understand, to rethink it, then paint it or dance it, and when it's over we still have to sort through it, look back, reset to zero, and start again.

So I think our most precious moments may not be the few minutes on stage at all. They're in how we reset before we go up, how we reset after we come down, and where God is in between. I don't want to give you a correct answer, and I hope none of us turn into people who hand out correct answers. Let's just think about it together, and think about what we can leave for the people coming after us.
