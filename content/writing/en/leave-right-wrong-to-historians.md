That day we saved a little time at the end of class for Q&A. One student raised a hand and said that the first time they saw all those camera feeds on the screens, their first thought was, this is kind of like a God's-eye view. What we get to see is decided by you guys. So don't you need to be even closer to God to know what to show us? It feels like such a heavy responsibility, like being another preacher.

I agreed completely. A more experienced colleague teaching with me added that when you're directing, there really are a lot of voices in your ears. Besides talking to the camera operators on headset, other people are talking to you on the intercom, there are several cameras plus a drone, and trying to get yourself into worship gets interrupted a lot. Someone else said it was only when a sermon video moved them that they realized somebody had made it possible for that to reach them, and that just sitting there in the room, they might not even have caught that line. Another student said this was the closest they'd ever been to this kind of work.

## We're all just pipes

I thought about it and told everyone I think we're actually all the same. Dancers, painters, people holding cameras: we're all channels for God, reflecting His glory through our bodies, through what we paint, through what we shoot. So instead of constantly asking myself whether I've correctly conveyed what God means, the way I tend to see it is that I'm just what His light looks like when it's reflected, and as for how the people around me receive it, God will tune that frequency. Of course, we still have to do our part: keep ourselves aimed at Him, keep our hearts pure, keep ourselves a little cleaner. That might give you more confidence to be the pipe.

But I don't really want to look at this in terms of right and wrong. Once we take back our identity as God's children, it seems like the question of right and wrong goes away, and there's only one line left: "Dad, what do you want me to do?" When you do it, there will be a result, and that result isn't right or wrong either. It's just a result. Right and wrong is what historians talk about, so let's leave it to the historians. In the moment, we just go do it. I think each of us is an actor in God's play.

At the end we prayed together. I remember saying something like: Lord, let us not chase right and wrong. What we chase is Your presence.
