I'm usually the one behind the camera. This time I was the one sitting in the control room at Bread of Life Christian Church in Taipei, with the switcher and a whole wall of monitors behind me, being filmed for the church's *Our Family's Story* series. The four-minute film is called (translated) "Twenty Years of Serving, and the People Who Walked With Me," and the first title card asks: looking back on these 20 years, why did you join a serving team in the first place?

[▶ Watch the film on YouTube (Mandarin)](https://youtu.be/JO-8hbnEFX4)

## Back then, everyone was expected to serve

I remember a phrase that was everywhere at church back then: being part of the church doesn't just mean belonging to a small group, it also means having a place to serve. Once I started attending regularly, that idea really pulled me in. I thought, oh, so this is what the ideal Christian life looks like. And since I'd just graduated in film, I could use my own training to serve God. That sounded like fun.

What I'm most grateful for about the media team is how much freedom it gave me. I could take what I'd learned at school and use it directly. If it worked smoothly, great. If it had a few holes, we adjusted. The strange part is that God seemed to have it all lined up. A problem I ran into at church would often show up again at work right after. A new project would land at work, and I'd look back and see the church had a need related to it. So I'd take what I'd already worked through on the job and bring it back to church. Work and serving ended up feeding each other, and it felt a bit like pedaling a flywheel.

## When I was low, I still showed up every time

Film people all write scripts, so we know instinctively that people have highs where everything goes right, and lows that are guaranteed to come. But when the low actually happens to you, you barely notice it. During that stretch I couldn't get excited about anything. I just wanted to stay home, and I didn't really want to see anyone.

But I didn't stop serving because of the low, or the depression. When I came back to church I made God a small promise: from now on, I'll do my best to show up for every one of our appointments. And I did show up. I just couldn't control my mood or my face, and I probably hurt quite a few people on the team during that time.

It was also because I kept staying in that community that people kept giving me strength. Slowly I understood that what we give in this family, others really do feel, and they give back, in very practical ways. That showed me the relationships between us were real. Since I became a Christian, apart from my wife, the people who have walked through the most of my life with me are this group I serve alongside.

## Last year, my whole family worked a service together

We have two kids, and they keep getting bigger. Last year we finally had the chance to work a service together as a family: their mom sat at the switcher, and both kids picked up cameras. My mom raised me on her own, so as a kid I never really got to feel what it's like for a whole family to do something together. For me, that service was beautiful, comfortable and a lot of fun.

Looking back, that came from church too. In that community we learned that relationships are about respect and freedom, and I've tried to bring that home. If we're doing God's work, shouldn't we do it gladly and willingly? The process might be painful, hard, sad or uncomfortable, but taken as a whole, when we look back, if the road was joyful and willing, I believe God will be pleased with it.

At the end of the film I said I was really looking forward to this story being released. It's a new storyline I'd love to see: people connecting with each other into one big community, growing together and moving forward together.

If you want to know how I first ended up at church, I wrote about [the classmate who took me there](/en/writing/the-classmate-who-took-me-to-church). Thank you to the Bread of Life team for filming, and to everyone who has called cues with me in that control room for 20 years. Four minutes is short, so if you have time, go watch the whole thing on YouTube.
