## Stringing the plot together: it makes everything possible

If you don't realize this is adapted from a classic children's book, you might miss the chance to enjoy all the author's clever touches. You might say it's a story made by piling together every plot idea someone had, with each part sprouting the hooks it needs to pull the next one along. Not forced, exactly, but a little deliberate.

### Straight to number one

Netflix's *The Magician's Elephant* came out on March 17, 2023 and went straight to number one in Taiwan's Kids section, flattening every other big title on the chart! With a banner on the home page too, anyone browsing the Kids section would find it hard not to click in for three to five minutes!

![Netflix Taiwan Kids chart with The Magician's Elephant at number one](/writing/vocus/the-magicians-elephant/01.jpg)

### Artwork with incredible detail

Once you start, though, you might not be able to stop, and you'll be secretly glad it's a movie and not eight or nine episodes that eat your whole day! Before the story has given you much to go on, take a close look at the craft in the artwork. The texture of the main character's clothes, and the way wind, clouds, fire and other mysterious things in the environment affect everything, are all drawn in fine, readable detail. I'd recommend watching it on the best screen you own. The depth and detail are worth enjoying as pure eye candy!

![Still from The Magician's Elephant, the boy hugging the elephant's trunk](/writing/vocus/the-magicians-elephant/02.jpg)

### Guess who's playing who

And then there are the characters. I suspect every one of them was drawn with some Netflix star, or some actor working right now, in mind, and a few even reminded me of friends I actually know! I'd love to run a guessing game: which star did the artists base each character on?

![Characters from The Magician's Elephant: the girl, the boy and the painted elephant](/writing/vocus/the-magicians-elephant/03.jpg)

### Make the impossible possible

As for the core idea: if you don't have the literary background, you'll probably end up like me, muttering at how deliberately every plot point connects to the next. Not far-fetched, mind you. Deliberate, so the audience gets hints and sees things coming.

Then I did a little field research and found out it's adapted from a novel! All I can say is, my apologies. I'll find the book and read it one of these days, but I'm sure it played out like this because adapting it meant some very hard choices about what to keep and what to cut.

![English cover of the original The Magician's Elephant book](/writing/vocus/the-magicians-elephant/04.jpg)

And I think that fits the film's core idea too: "Everything is possible."

## The Magician's Elephant

A determined boy takes on a king's challenge: complete three impossible tasks to win a magical elephant, and the chance to go after his own destiny.

![Netflix poster for The Magician's Elephant, the boy sitting against the elephant](/writing/vocus/the-magicians-elephant/05.jpg)

### A story where everyone changes

In this film, every character is given a really important job in moving the story forward. All the supporting characters gather around the hero and help him shine!

A story driven by its characters like this is perfect for parents and kids to watch together. You can comfortably talk as you watch about how each character is feeling, and how those feelings change.

Speaking of change, it must really come from being adapted from literature, because you rarely see a script where every single character goes through an inner shift: from doubt to belief, from fear to courage, from refusal to acceptance, and so on. If you pay attention to how the people in it change, I'm certain you'll find it very skillfully done!

So my viewing guide for this one: settle in and really notice how the characters change!

### Impossible // Possible

Once that rhythm is set, the whole film keeps flipping around that core idea. Each challenge the hero faces lays out the story's central question very directly and moves the timeline along. The second thread, the magician and the lady, wrestles with what magic is really for and what it means. The mystery of the boy's past strengthens the central question and pulls the two storylines together.

The whole film is tight, and it spells out its message plainly, even bluntly. Very much the Netflix original animation style. It's great for helping kids build positive values and an eye for visual beauty. The artwork really is fantastic!

### What if

There's a character in the film who loves to think in "What if…," and he's my personal favorite. He asks questions that break out of the box, tries experiments, and even encourages and helps the kid he's in a position to help!

Personally, I think the character's look is based on former US President Barack Obama, and I kept making up my own little side plot while I watched.

![Still from The Magician's Elephant, the guard captain with his hand on his chest](/writing/vocus/the-magicians-elephant/06.jpg)

He's the captain of the guard, the bridge between the boy and the people who run the city, so of course he's a really important character for moving things forward.

And later in the film, whenever things come back down to hard reality, his "What if…" philosophy turns out to be exactly what's needed. That way of thinking finds another possible way forward for every difficulty in the film! Maybe he's the real hero of the story's central question!

## Go a day without eating, or go your whole life without knowing?

It sounds like a deadly serious threat, but it's the key that gets the whole film started. In our real lives, from being born to going to school to finding our feet in the world, we might spend our days drifting, and also searching for our calling, something we can spend a whole lifetime romantically chasing!

So let me ask you: would you make that choice? I quietly asked myself, and deep down there's a fear there. The fear is something like, "What if I find out, and it's too late?" Or, "What if I've known all along, and there's nothing I can do about it?"

Then I suddenly thought of the crystal merchant in *The Alchemist*… For a moment I couldn't say whether that's good or bad. Maybe, like the shepherd boy, the most beautiful part is the whole journey, not the final result. The calling we think we have is really found in the process of living it out, and that process is the calling itself. I think the magician in this film would say the same.

It's a good one to watch together as a family. Remember to talk about it afterwards!

## Questions to talk about

Q: Whose story did you like best? If it were you, which character would you want to be?

Q: If you were Mr. What If, would you believe the boy upstairs the way he does?

Q: If you were the officer who took the boy in, would you be willing to admit you'd misunderstood?

Q: If you were the boy, how would you solve the third challenge? Show us!
